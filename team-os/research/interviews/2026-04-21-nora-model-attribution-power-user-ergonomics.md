## Account context

Nora is an independent research consultant and one of the most AI-native users in the broader customer base. This call focused less on activation and more on what keeps a highly technical user invested versus what would make them plateau.

## Transcript

**[00:00] Priya:** I wanted to understand what keeps you using the product versus what still feels underpowered.

**[00:09] Nora:** The main thing keeping me is control. Bring-your-own-key, model choice, provider choice. Those are not side details for me.

**[00:19] Priya:** Do you actually change providers often?

**[00:22] Nora:** Enough that it matters. More importantly, I like knowing the product is not hiding the model layer from me.

**[00:30] Priya:** So explicitness is part of the value.

**[00:33] Nora:** Exactly.

**[00:35] Priya:** What still feels weak?

**[00:38] Nora:** Attribution and agent ergonomics. I can choose a model, but once I have a bunch of outputs and experiments, I want clearer visibility into what produced what.

**[00:49] Priya:** Is that a mass-market issue?

**[00:51] Nora:** Probably not first. It is more of a power-user retention issue.

**[00:56] Priya:** That distinction helps.

**[00:58] Nora:** It should. I would not want the team to confuse my preferences with the whole market.

**[01:04] Priya:** What about Squad specifically?

**[01:07] Nora:** Strong concept, high setup cost. The product asks me to design my own process pretty early.

**[01:15] Priya:** Would better defaults help?

**[01:18] Nora:** A lot. Starter agents, clearer examples, better visibility into what tools the agents actually use.

**[01:26] Priya:** So more guidance, not less ambition.

**[01:29] Nora:** Exactly.

**[01:49] Priya:** That is useful because it keeps us from overweighting loud advanced users.

**[01:54] Nora:** Right. The advanced users are often directionally helpful, but not always priority-helpful.

**[02:01] Priya:** If I bring one phrase back from this call, what should it be?

**[02:29] Priya:** So the delight is partly architectural for you.

**[02:32] Nora:** Very much. It feels like a real system, not just a chatbot glued onto notes.

**[02:44] Nora:** Exactly.

**[02:46] Priya:** Does that frustration weaken retention or just limit enthusiasm?

**[03:12] Nora:** Thanks.

**[03:31] Priya:** So more confidence in reuse.

**[03:34] Nora:** Exactly. Reuse is where systems either become real or stay toys.

**[03:39] Priya:** And you still would not confuse that with the main adoption unlock for the broader market.

**[03:44] Nora:** Correct. That is what makes the prioritization tricky but manageable.

**[04:27] Priya:** That is a helpful split.

**[04:34] Priya:** Nice phrase.

**[04:36] Nora:** It is true, though.

**[04:56] Nora:** Exactly.

**[04:58] Priya:** What makes the frustration sharper instead of merely academic?

**[05:17] Nora:** Yes.

**[05:24] Nora:** Yes. I am more willing to experiment than to rely on it. That is a big difference.

**[05:34] Nora:** Exactly.

**[05:36] Priya:** What would move it across that threshold?

**[05:39] Nora:** Better inspectability, stronger defaults, and clearer understanding of what the agent can touch in the workspace. Basically: let me trust reuse.

**[05:49] Priya:** Last question. If I summarized this account to the team in one line beyond the quote, what would be fair?

**[05:55] Nora:** The current app already feels unusually serious for AI-native users because control is real. The missing maturity layer is not capability but confidence in repeated use.

**[06:09] Priya:** If I asked what would make the product feel one maturity level higher, what is the shortest answer?

**[06:14] Nora:** Better inspectability around repeatable workflows.

**[06:18] Priya:** Meaning traces, defaults, and clearer boundaries.

**[06:21] Nora:** Exactly. Enough that I can trust reuse instead of just admire potential.

**[06:27] Priya:** Do you think that matters only to experts?

**[06:30] Nora:** The full version matters mostly to experts. But the downstream effect matters more broadly, because if power users cannot stabilize workflows, they cannot model good usage for everyone else.

**[06:42] Priya:** That is a useful account-level argument.

**[06:45] Nora:** It is the difference between a feature being impressive and a feature becoming an operating habit.

**[06:56] Nora:** Yes. Just at a different layer of the product than the beginner complaints.

**[07:26] Priya:** That is a strong description of the value.

**[07:29] Nora:** It is why I still care. The fundamentals are very good.

**[07:33] Priya:** And where does that day start to wobble?

**[07:36] Nora:** The moment I want to turn the ad hoc flow into a repeatable one. Then I need more than power. I need inspectability, confidence, and some sense that the system will behave the same way next time.

**[07:50] Priya:** So the friction is not first-use friction. It is repeat-use friction.

**[07:54] Nora:** Exactly. That is the retention layer.

**[07:57] Priya:** Do you think the team could misread your behavior because you keep using the product anyway?

**[08:02] Nora:** Absolutely. Continued usage can hide the fact that I am not deepening reliance at the pace I would if the agent layer felt more trustworthy.

**[08:14] Priya:** So the risk is not that you disappear. It is that you plateau.

**[08:18] Nora:** Yes. Plateau is the right word.

**[08:21] Priya:** What would tell you that plateau had broken?

**[08:24] Nora:** If I started reusing the same agent patterns with confidence instead of recreating or rethinking them each time.

**[08:33] Priya:** That sounds measurable in behavior terms.

**[08:36] Nora:** It is. Reuse is the signal.

**[08:39] Priya:** Does that also affect how credible the product feels when you recommend it to other sophisticated users?

**[08:45] Nora:** Definitely. Right now I recommend it as a serious and promising system. I would rather recommend it as a system with repeatable trust.

**[08:56] Priya:** That is a big difference in tone.

**[08:58] Nora:** Huge difference.

**[09:00] Priya:** What should the team not do in response to a call like this?

**[09:16] Priya:** That is a very useful warning.

**[09:18] Nora:** Good. Because advanced-user feedback often gets mistranslated into "add more controls," when the real ask is usually "make the current system more understandable."

**[09:30] Priya:** If the team solved this well, what would you say six months from now?

**[09:43] Priya:** Last question for real. What is the fair one-line business readout on this account?

**[09:49] Nora:** The current app already wins advanced-user respect; the missing layer is what turns that respect into repeatable operational trust.
