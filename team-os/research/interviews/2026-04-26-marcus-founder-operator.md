## Quick context

Marcus is the founder of a small B2B services startup. He uses Noted heavily for strategy drafts and investor/customer updates, but he rarely publishes directly. He has tried Squad agents twice and bounced both times because setup felt like a separate project.

## Transcript

**[00:00] Sarah:** Thanks for doing this. I saw you have a lot of Coworker activity and very few published pages, so I want to understand what is happening between those two.

**[00:10] Marcus:** That is fair. I use Noted a lot. I just do not trust myself to publish from it without another pass.

**[00:18] Sarah:** Another pass from you, or someone else?

**[00:21] Marcus:** Usually someone else. My cofounder or our fractional marketer. I need somebody to say, "this makes sense" before it becomes official.

**[00:32] Sarah:** What kind of docs are these?

**[00:34] Marcus:** Investor updates, customer follow-ups, internal strategy notes, sometimes hiring scorecards. The AI helps me get the skeleton out. The risky part is tone and implication.

**[00:47] Sarah:** What is risky about tone?

**[00:49] Marcus:** Founder writing carries weight. If I publish a note, people assume it is a decision. Sometimes I am still thinking.

**[00:58] Sarah:** So publish changes the meaning of the draft.

**[01:01] Marcus:** Exactly. It goes from "help me think" to "this is what I believe." That jump is bigger than the UI makes it feel.

**[01:12] Sarah:** How do you handle that jump today?

**[01:15] Marcus:** I copy the doc into Slack or email and ask for reactions. Which is silly because the whole reasoning trail is in Noted.

**[01:24] Sarah:** Reasoning trail meaning the Coworker conversation?

**[01:27] Marcus:** Yes, plus the rough notes. If my marketer asks why I framed something a certain way, the answer is usually in the conversation I had with the AI ten minutes earlier.

**[01:38] Sarah:** Do you want the reviewer to read that conversation?

**[01:41] Marcus:** Selectively. Not all of it. Some of it is messy or private. But I would love to expose "source context" without exposing the whole scratchpad.

**[01:54] Sarah:** That is more nuanced than a simple shared doc.

**[01:57] Marcus:** Yes. A shared doc solves edit access. It does not solve judgment context.

**[02:05] Sarah:** You mentioned before the call that you tried Squad agents. What happened?

**[02:09] Marcus:** I wanted an "investor update coach." It sounded perfect. Then I got to the setup and realized I had to write instructions for a thing that was supposed to help me write.

**[02:22] Sarah:** That felt circular.

**[02:24] Marcus:** Very. I know what a good investor update feels like, but I do not know how to describe an agent well enough to get there.

**[02:34] Sarah:** Did you abandon it?

**[02:36] Marcus:** Twice. The second time I got farther, but then I wondered what tools it should have and whether it could see the right docs. At that point I went back to Coworker.

**[02:49] Sarah:** So Coworker wins because it is immediate.

**[02:52] Marcus:** Yes. It is low ceremony. Squad feels powerful but asks for configuration before I have confidence.

**[03:02] Sarah:** What would have helped?

**[03:04] Marcus:** A starting agent that already knows the job. "Investor update reviewer." "Customer follow-up drafter." Let me edit from there.

**[03:14] Sarah:** Would you connect that to collaboration?

**[03:17] Marcus:** Actually yes. The best version is: Coworker helps draft, a reviewer or agent pressure-tests it, then I approve it for publishing.

**[03:28] Sarah:** You used the word approve.

**[03:30] Marcus:** That is the real missing step for me. I do not need multiplayer editing. I need a lightweight approval loop before a doc leaves the workshop.

**[03:41] Sarah:** Who approves?

**[03:43] Marcus:** Depends. For investor updates, my cofounder. For customer emails, marketer. For internal strategy, maybe just me after a delay.

**[03:54] Sarah:** Delay?

**[03:56] Marcus:** Sometimes I want the product to force a pause. "You drafted this with AI twenty minutes ago; are you sure you want to publish?" That might sound annoying, but it would save me from myself.

**[04:09] Sarah:** Is that because of AI trust?

**[04:11] Marcus:** Partly. I trust AI for shape, not commitments. It is great at producing a confident paragraph. That is exactly why I need a review step.

**[04:22] Sarah:** Does model attribution matter to you?

**[04:25] Marcus:** More than I expected. If a paragraph came mostly from AI, I want to know before I publish. Not because AI is bad, but because I need to own it differently.

**[04:38] Sarah:** Would you expect the reviewer to know that too?

**[04:41] Marcus:** Yes. If my cofounder sees a section was AI-assisted, they will review it differently. They might ask, "is this actually our claim?"

**[04:52] Sarah:** That sounds like provenance.

**[04:54] Marcus:** Exactly. I do not need a forensic audit. I need enough signal to slow down where the risk is higher.

**[05:04] Sarah:** Let me ask about publishing. Have you published anything from Noted?

**[05:08] Marcus:** A few things. Mostly low-stakes pages: resources, a public note, maybe a hiring blurb. The important docs usually leave Noted first.

**[05:20] Sarah:** Why?

**[05:22] Marcus:** Because outside tools have the social workflow. Comments, approvals, someone saying "ship it." Noted has the thinking workflow.

**[05:32] Sarah:** That is the gap.

**[05:34] Marcus:** Yes. Thinking to shipping.

**[05:37] Sarah:** If we shipped comments first, would that help?

**[05:40] Marcus:** Yes, if comments had a sense of resolution. I do not want a bunch of loose feedback sitting there forever. I want "resolved, ready, approved."

**[05:52] Sarah:** So not just comments. Comments plus a decision state.

**[05:56] Marcus:** Exactly. Even a tiny "approved by Marcus" or "approved by cofounder" would matter.

**[06:04] Sarah:** What about roles: viewer, commenter, editor, admin?

**[06:21] Sarah:** Would you invite external reviewers?

**[06:34] Sarah:** What would make you not use it?

**[06:36] Marcus:** If I cannot tell what they can see. If they can see the AI conversation by accident, I am out.

**[06:45] Sarah:** Similar to what Rina told me.

**[06:47] Marcus:** That does not surprise me. Draft context is valuable and dangerous.

**[06:54] Sarah:** How often do you hit this review-before-publish step?

**[07:09] Sarah:** Does the lack of this step reduce how much you use Noted?

**[07:25] Sarah:** What metric would show that working?

**[08:07] Sarah:** If you had to pick one slice for us to build first, what would it be?

**[08:11] Marcus:** Lightweight review link: comment-only, visible AI provenance cues, and an approve button. Not full collaboration. A bridge.
